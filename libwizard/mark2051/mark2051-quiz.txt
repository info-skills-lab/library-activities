MARK2051 Check your learning: LibWizard quiz
============================================

How it's set up
- Add these to slide 10 in LibWizard, in this order, with a Page Break between each.
- Multiple choice questions: Radios. In Answer Properties, set the correct answer (✓) and tick Display correct/wrong messages.
- To make every question compulsory, tick Required on each question. Students can't continue until they answer it.
- Leave Require correct answer to continue unticked, so a wrong answer doesn't stop them.
- Confidence ratings: Rating field, Radio button type, Horizontal, Answer set to Not Selected. Tick Required on these too if you want them compulsory.
- Settings: answers table on, retake off.
- The quiz end points students to Finish.
- Each question is tagged with its Digital Information Skills Framework area and source slide for your reference. Don't add the tags to LibWizard.


PART A. BEFORE YOU START (rating scale, 1 to 5)
-----------------------------------------------
Scale: 1 Not at all confident, 5 Very confident.
Prompt: How confident are you that you can do the following?

A1. Break down a research question into keywords and a database search.
A2. Find peer-reviewed research and consumer data in Library databases.
A3. Evaluate a source using the CRAAP test.
A4. Use AI tools and the information you find responsibly.


PART B. QUESTIONS (12)
----------------------

1. Which words are the key concepts in "How can Microsoft build trust and encourage responsible adoption of Microsoft Copilot among consumers?"
   a) trust, adoption, Microsoft Copilot, consumers ✓
   b) how, build, encourage, among
   c) Microsoft, responsible, how
   d) consumers only
   Correct: Correct. These are the ideas you search for. Words like "how" and "build" guide your answer but aren't searched.
   Wrong: That’s incorrect. Key concepts are the main ideas you search for. Words like "how" and "build" aren't searched.
   [Identify · Slide 2]

2. You need figures showing how often Gen Z consumers use generative AI. Which source fits best?
   a) Consumer survey data in Passport GMID ✓
   b) A peer-reviewed article on why people trust technology
   c) A Microsoft press release
   d) An opinion piece in a newspaper
   Correct: Correct. Survey data measures what consumers do, and Passport lets you compare segments.
   Wrong: That’s incorrect. For figures on what a segment does, look for survey data, such as in Passport GMID.
   [Identify · Slide 5]

3. You have three peer-reviewed articles explaining why consumers trust AI, but no data on your segment. What should you do next?
   a) Look for survey data on your segment ✓
   b) Stop, as you have enough sources
   c) Find more articles making the same point
   d) Ask an AI tool to estimate the figures
   Correct: Correct. Research explains why. Data shows what your segment does. You need both.
   Wrong: That’s incorrect. Research explains why, but you still need data on what your segment does.
   [Identify · Slides 4 and 5]

4. Which search will find the most results?
   a) trust AND "generative AI" AND consumer*
   b) (trust OR confidence OR credibility) AND "generative AI" ✓
   c) trust AND confidence AND "generative AI"
   d) "trust in generative AI among consumers"
   Correct: Correct. OR adds synonyms and widens the search, and fewer AND concepts keep it broad.
   Wrong: That’s incorrect. OR widens a search. Each extra AND, or a long phrase in quotation marks, narrows it.
   [Discover · Slide 2]

5. What does consumer* find?
   a) consumer, consumers and consumerism ✓
   b) Only consumer and consumers
   c) Synonyms such as customer and shopper
   d) The exact phrase consumer*
   Correct: Correct. Truncation finds every ending, so check your results for words you didn't want.
   Wrong: That’s incorrect. The asterisk finds every word ending, including some you may not want, like consumerism.
   [Discover · Slide 2]

6. Your Business Source Premier search returns 4,000 results. What narrows it most effectively?
   a) Add another concept with AND, and use the peer-reviewed and date filters ✓
   b) Add more synonyms with OR
   c) Remove the quotation marks from your phrases
   d) Run the same search in Google
   Correct: Correct. AND and filters, such as peer reviewed and publication date, both narrow your results to the most relevant.
   Wrong: That’s incorrect. OR and removing quotation marks both widen a search. Use AND and filters to narrow it.
   [Discover · Slide 3]

7. When is a natural language search most useful?
   a) When you're new to a topic and unsure of the keywords ✓
   b) When you need a precise search you can repeat exactly
   c) When you want only peer-reviewed results
   d) When the database has no filters
   Correct: Correct. A natural language search is a good start, and you can find other relevant keywords from its results.
   Wrong: That’s incorrect. A natural language search suits getting started. A Boolean search is more precise and repeatable.
   [Discover · Slide 3]

8. You want to know when new articles on consumer trust in AI are published. What should you do?
   a) Save your search in the database and set an alert ✓
   b) Run the same Google search each week
   c) Ask an AI tool to tell you about new research
   d) Wait until your lecturer mentions new articles
   Correct: Correct. An alert emails you new results for your saved search.
   Wrong: That’s incorrect. Databases like Scopus can save your search and email you new results.
   [Discover · Slide 4]

9. A report on AI adoption is funded by an AI software company. Which part of the CRAAP test raises a concern?
   a) Purpose ✓
   b) Currency
   c) Relevance
   d) Authority
   Correct: Correct. The funder benefits from good news about AI, so check for bias.
   Wrong: That’s incorrect. Think about why the report was written, and who benefits.
   [Evaluate · Slide 8]

10. A peer-reviewed study looks at trust in AI among retirees in the United States. Your segment is Australian university students. Which part of the CRAAP test does it fail?
   a) Relevance ✓
   b) Authority
   c) Accuracy
   d) It passes every check, as it's peer reviewed
   Correct: Correct. Peer review doesn't make a source relevant to your question.
   Wrong: That’s incorrect. It's peer reviewed, but a different country and age group.
   [Evaluate · Slide 8]

11. An AI tool tells you "80% of Australians trust AI" but gives no source. What should you do?
   a) Find the original source in a Library database or another trusted source before you use it ✓
   b) Use it, and cite the AI tool as the source
   c) Use it, as AI tools are usually accurate
   d) Ask the AI tool if the figure is correct
   Correct: Correct. AI can produce figures that are wrong or made up. Always trace them to a reliable source.
   Wrong: That’s incorrect. AI can make up figures, so check it against a reliable source first.
   [Evaluate · Slide 9]

12. Your course allows AI. You rewrite a paragraph Copilot generated into your own words. What should you do?
   a) Acknowledge your AI use as your course requires ✓
   b) Nothing, as you've rewritten it in your own words
   c) Cite the paragraph as your own work
   d) Delete your earlier drafts
   Correct: Correct. Rewritten AI content still needs acknowledging.
   Wrong: That’s incorrect. Rewriting AI content in your own words still requires proper attribution.
   [Use responsibly · Slide 9]
