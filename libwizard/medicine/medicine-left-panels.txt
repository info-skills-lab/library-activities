========== 1. Welcome to Medicine HDR Essentials ==========

<h3 style="border-bottom:4px solid #ffe000; padding-bottom:6px">Welcome to Medicine HDR Essentials</h3><p>This tutorial shows you how to find, search and evaluate medical research using the Library collection. The research question on the right is used throughout.</p><p>You’ll learn to</p><ul><li><strong>Break down</strong> a research question into concepts and synonyms</li><li><strong>Combine</strong> keywords with AND and OR</li><li><strong>Search</strong> Scopus, Web of Science and PubMed, including MeSH headings</li><li><strong>Refine</strong> a search that has too many or too few results</li><li><strong>Evaluate</strong> sources and use AI tools responsibly</li></ul><h4>What to do</h4><ol><li>Read the research question on the right.</li><li>Answer the question about therapy studies.</li></ol><p>Your progress shows in the black bar at the top of each activity. ========== 2. Medicine & health Subject Guide ==========

<h3 style="border-bottom:4px solid #ffe000; padding-bottom:6px">Medicine &amp; health Subject Guide</h3><p>The Library’s subject guides are a great starting point for discovering databases relevant to your research. ========== 3. Breaking down the question ==========

<h3 style="border-bottom:4px solid #ffe000; padding-bottom:6px">Breaking down the question</h3><p>Before you search, break your question into its key concepts. Then think of the different words authors might use for each one.</p><h4>What to do</h4><ol><li>Watch the video on the right.</li><li>In Activity 1, <strong>Find the concepts</strong>, choose a tag, then select the words in the question. Select <strong>Check</strong>.</li><li>In Activity 2, <strong>Choose synonyms</strong>, select the synonyms you would add for each concept. Your search builds as you choose.</li></ol><p><a href="https://info-skills-lab.github.io/library-activities/medicine-research.html?part=2" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Open the breaking down the question activity in a new window</a></p><div style="background:#d9edf7; border:1px solid #bce8f1; border-radius:4px; color:#1f4e66; margin:10px 0 0 0; padding:10px 14px"><p style="margin:0"><strong>Tip:</strong> Task words such as discuss tell you what to do. They aren’t search terms.</p></div>

========== 7. Database search ==========

<h3 style="border-bottom:4px solid #ffe000; padding-bottom:6px">Database search</h3><p>Scopus and Web of Science are two of the most widely used multidisciplinary databases in academic research. ========== 8. Searching PubMed ==========

<h3 style="border-bottom:4px solid #ffe000; padding-bottom:6px">Searching PubMed</h3><p>PubMed indexes biomedical and health literature. Its records are tagged with MeSH (Medical Subject Headings), a controlled vocabulary that brings together the different words authors use for the same idea.</p><h4>What to do</h4><ol><li>At yellow number <strong>1</strong>, look up echinacea, then common cold. Add each MeSH heading to your search.</li><li>At <strong>2</strong>, choose the MeSH headings and keywords for each concept.</li><li>At <strong>3</strong>, limit to therapy studies.</li><li>Select <strong>Search PubMed</strong> to run your search for real.</li></ol><p><a href="https://info-skills-lab.github.io/library-activities/medicine-research.html?part=7" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Open the PubMed activity in a new window</a></p><div style="background:#d9edf7; border:1px solid #bce8f1; border-radius:4px; color:#1f4e66; margin:10px 0 0 0; padding:10px 14px"><p style="margin:0"><strong>Tip:</strong> Search MeSH and keywords together. The newest articles may not have MeSH headings yet.</p></div><div style="background:#f5f5f5; border:1px solid #e3e3e3; border-radius:4px; margin:10px 0 0 0; padding:10px 14px; font-size:0.9em"><p style="margin:0"><strong>Sources:</strong> National Library of Medicine, MeSH records for Echinacea and Common Cold; University of North Carolina Health Sciences Library, PubMed filters guide.</p></div>
